For years I only ever saw black holes as images: the blurry Event Horizon Telescope photo, _Interstellar_'s Gargantua, the odd render. I wanted something different — a black hole you could **touch**: rotate, zoom, change — and that was **real physics**, not a graphical effect.

So I built a [black hole simulator](/en/lab/black-hole) that runs in real time in the browser, free, with nothing to install.

## I don't draw it, I compute it

That's the whole difference. For every single pixel on screen the program shoots a ray of light and lets it fall through the curved spacetime around the black hole, following the metric's **exact null geodesic** — the same path a real photon takes, obtained by numerically solving the equations of general relativity.

From that one act, everything you see emerges **on its own**: the bent light (gravitational lensing), the photon sphere, the central shadow and the thin photon ring. None of it is drawn by hand.

## What you can do

- **Spin the black hole** with the Spin slider: it's not a trick, it's the **exact Kerr metric** ray-traced in real time — the same one as _Interstellar_'s Gargantua, which was rendered offline at hours per frame. The asymmetric shadow and real frame-dragging appear.
- **The accretion disk** glows with the true blackbody colour of its temperature, warped by the relativistic Doppler effect: one side blinding, the other dim.
- **EHT** mode mimics the real Event Horizon Telescope photo; **Starless** mode turns the background into a Milky Way lensed by the hole.
- In the [Playground](/en/lab/black-hole/playground) you can throw stars, planets and comets and watch them be **torn apart** (spaghettification) in the tidal field, with gravitational waves and mergers.
- The [Orbits](/en/lab/black-hole/orbit) demo integrates the exact relativistic orbits — periastron precession, ISCO — with a live conservation diagnostic.

## Honesty first

What is real physics is real physics; what is approximate is **stated openly**. The lensing and the orbits are exact; the disk is a model (no magnetohydrodynamics — that would take hours per frame). The [equations page](/en/lab/black-hole/about) has all the maths with sources, and the [FAQ](/en/lab/black-hole/faq) answers the questions everyone asks — from "what happens if you fall in" to "does time really slow down".

No hidden tricks. It's a real black hole, computed pixel by pixel, in your browser.
