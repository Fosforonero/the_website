SplitVote is a tool for group decisions: "where do we eat tonight?", "which logo do we go with?", "should we vote yes on this proposal?". 80% of the use cases are one-off, inside a group chat, with people who will never come back to the app.

That's why there's no account system.

## What this means in practice

When you create a poll:

1. You get a link with a random slug
2. You automatically become the admin of that poll (local cookie)
3. You share the link in chat
4. Whoever clicks, votes — no sign-up

For voters: identity comes from a lightweight browser fingerprint, with no IP logged server-side. A user can only vote once per poll, but if they switch browsers they can vote again. That's an acceptable tradeoff: SplitVote isn't a tool for elections, it's a tool for friends.

## Problems this solved for me

- **Zero emails to send**: no verification, no password reset, no Supabase Auth triggers to debug.
- **Zero compliance overhead**: no accounts = no personal data = no GDPR concerns beyond the strictly-necessary cookie.
- **Clean funnel**: from click to vote in 2 seconds. Conversion rate is far higher than comparable tools.

## Problems it created

- **Account recovery**: if you lose the admin cookie, you lose control of the poll. Fix: the admin can download a "recovery token" at creation time.
- **Spam protection**: without accounts, bots have an easier time gaming the vote. For now an aggressive rate limit at the edge does the job.
- **Mobile onboarding**: in the mobile version I'm building, I'll need to add optional accounts for users who want to keep their polls over time.

> The right question isn't "do I need an account system?". It's "what's the first drop-off point, and how do I remove it?".
